# Reply on the HC2 issue to "Have you encountered this case on any real data yourself?"

Thanks for looking at it, and sorry for the length: the short version is the code sample and the four lines under "Why".

To answer the question honestly: the report was written from synthetic BAMs. Since you asked, I ran the same check on two real samples, both public 1000 Genomes BWA alignments of HG00096 (chromosome 20 only, bwa 0.5.9 `aln`/`sampe`), counted against Ensembl GRCh37 genes with `htseq-count -r pos -f bam -s no` and the default `-a 10`:

| sample | pairs with exactly one mate unmapped | of which the mapped mate has MAPQ ≥ 10 | `__too_low_aQual` on `main` | with the fix | genes whose count changes (of genes with count > 0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exome (1.93 M paired records) | 3,195 | 2,840 | 6,574 | 3,734 | 437 of 1,163 |
| low coverage (2.92 M) | 7,247 | 5,935 | 24,655 | 18,720 | 224 of 1,275 |

Every unmapped-mate record in both files has MAPQ 0, and the pairs the fix recovers are exactly the one-mate-unmapped pairs whose mapped mate passes the cutoff (2,840 and 5,935, to the pair). Per-gene changes are mostly under 1 %, up to 20 % on genes with single-digit counts.

So: real BWA output behaves exactly as the synthetic case, and on these two DNA samples the effect is small, about 0.3 % of pairs. I could not get a real RNA-seq run into the environment I have (ENA and SRA are not reachable from it), so I cannot say how large it is for a typical HISAT2 RNA-seq library, where the fraction of pairs with one unmapped mate is what sets the size. The script is in the audit repository (`audits/htseq/verify/hc2_real_1000g.py`) and downloads both samples from the public S3 bucket.
