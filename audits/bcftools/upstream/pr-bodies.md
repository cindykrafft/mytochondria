### PR 1 — `fix/mwu-bias-int-overflow` — "mpileup: compute the Mann-Whitney bias Z scores in 64 bits"

Fixes #NNN.

`calc_mwu_biasZ()` accumulates the tie correction, the U score and the group sizes in
`int`. The tie correction sums `p^3 - p` over the quality or position bins, where `p` is
the number of reads in a bin, so a single bin of 1291 reads takes the term past
`INT32_MAX`. The wrap is always downward, which inflates `var2` and shrinks the reported
|Z|: `INFO/MQBZ`, `BQBZ`, `RPBZ`, `SCBZ`, `MQSBZ` and `NMBZ` report a real bias as smaller
than it is, never larger.

The bias histograms are filled once per site across all input files
(`bcf_callaux_clean()` is called per site, not per sample) and `bam2bcf.c` clamps MQ and
BQ to bin 59, where most aligners put most reads — so the threshold is simply 1291 reads
at the site, which a 30-sample pileup at 100× already crosses. On a synthetic pileup of
3,000 reads over 30 files, `MQBZ` came out −6.88 where the tie-corrected Mann-Whitney Z
is −31.08. Sweeping realistic pileups, 41 of 160 changed their verdict under the
`MQBZ < -3` cut used in the calling workflow.

This makes `e`, `l`, `na`, `nb` and the per-bin `p` `int64_t`, which also removes the
`int` overflow of `na*nb` in the mean and variance at very high depth. No behaviour
changes below the threshold: the harness's control cases and the whole existing test
suite are unchanged.

Tests: `test/test-mwu.c`, in the style of `test/test-rbuf` and `test/test-regidx`, checks
`calc_mwu_biasZ()` at p = 1290 and p = 1291, on a 30-sample pileup and on a small pileup,
against the same statistic evaluated exactly. It fails on unmodified `develop` (2 of its
4 checks) and passes with this change; `make test` is 2480 passed / 0 failed either way
for the existing tests. Wired into `TEST_PROGRAMS` and both `check-*` targets.
