### PR 1 — `fix/stats-coverage-ring-buffer` — "stats: grow the coverage ring buffer instead of wrapping around"

Fixes #NNN.

`samtools stats` accumulates the coverage distribution (`COV` rows and the `-t`/`-g`
target percentage) in a ring buffer of `5 × nbases` cells indexed modulo its size
relative to the current read start. An aligned block ending more than that beyond the
read start — the far exon of a spliced alignment — wrapped around onto positions near
the read start. With 100-bp reads any intron longer than about 1.4 kb triggers it; on a
simulated six-exon gene the covered-position count was 18 % too high and the `-t`/`-g`
percentage was reported as 117.77 %.

This makes `round_buffer_insert_read()` grow the buffer (doubling, linearising the
pending counts) whenever a block would reach beyond it, through a new
`round_buffer_resize()`. The same helper replaces the copy in `realloc_buffers()`, which
passed element counts to `memcpy()` where byte counts were needed and dropped three
quarters of the pending counts whenever a longer read arrived while coverage was pending
near the end of the ring (mixed-length or long-read input).

Memory: the buffer now grows to the longest span between a read start and the end of
one of its blocks, so a 1-Mb intron costs 4 MB of `int`s; previously the size was fixed
at `5 × nbases`. A cap with a warning could be added if that is a concern.

Tests: `test/stat/20_spliced.sam` (the two-read wrap case) and
`test/stat/21_mixed_lengths.sam` (the reallocation case), compared on their `COV` rows;
both fail on unmodified `develop` and pass with this change. `make test` otherwise
unchanged (numbers in the audit's `upstream/README.md`).

NEWS entry included under "Bug fixes".

### PR 2 — `fix/stats-dup-supplementary-isize-sd` — "stats: count duplicates for the same records as "sequences"; include isize 0 in the SD"

Fixes #696.

Two small corrections to `samtools stats` SN numbers, one commit each, both in `stats.c`,
each with a regression test that fails on `develop`.

**Duplicate counts** (1776b17). `reads duplicated` and `bases duplicated` were accumulated
before the `IS_ORIGINAL()` gate in `collect_stats()`, so secondary and supplementary
records carrying the 0x400 flag were counted, while `sequences` and `total length` exclude
those records. A duplication rate formed from the two lines (MultiQC's samtools stats
module does this) was overstated once the supplementary records of duplicates were
flagged, which is what `samtools markdup -S` and Picard MarkDuplicates produce. This is
the move suggested in #696: the counting now happens inside the `IS_ORIGINAL()` block,
next to the read length counts, so the two pairs of lines refer to the same records. On
`test/stat/22_dup_supp.sam` (20 reads, 8 duplicates, 4 supplementary records belonging to
duplicate pairs) `develop` reports `reads duplicated: 10` and `bases duplicated: 860`
against `sequences: 20`; this branch reports 8 and 800. `flagstat` already distinguishes
`duplicates` from `primary duplicates`; `stats` had no such pair.

**Insert size standard deviation** (47d3376). The SD loop in `output_stats()` started at
`isize = 1`, while the mean and the count over the same bins started at 0, so pairs
reported with TLEN 0 entered the denominator but not the sum of squares and lowered the
SD instead of raising it. The loop now starts at 0, like the two loops before it. On
`test/stat/23_isize_zero.sam` (10 pairs, two with TLEN 0, the rest 300 to 360) `develop`
reports an SD of 60.1 against a mean of 259.0; the correct value for that mean is 130.5.
Whether the isize 0 bin belongs in these statistics at all is a separate question; this
change only makes the SD consistent with the mean printed next to it. Happy to drop this
commit if you would rather take it separately.

Tests: two new `test_cmd` entries in `test/test.pl` compare the SN lines named above; both
fail on unmodified `develop` (de749a6) and pass with this branch. `perl test/test.pl`:
1011 passed, 0 failed, 32 expected failures; `make test` passes. NEWS entries included
under "Bug fixes".
