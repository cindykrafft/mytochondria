Title: Per sequence quality scores: each read's mean quality is truncated toward zero before binning, so the histogram sits 0.5 too low on average

<!-- s-andrews/FastQC has no issue template. Second filing (with PR). Every version. -->

**Versions:** 0.11.5, 0.11.9, 0.12.1 and `master` @ 87fb336.

In `PerSequenceQualityScores.processSequence` the quality characters are summed into an `int` and divided by the read length with integer division:

```java
int averageQuality = 0;
for (...) { ... averageQuality += ch; }
if (qualLen > 0) {
    averageQuality /= qualLen;
```

A read whose mean quality is 27.9 is therefore counted in the 27 bin. The "Per sequence quality scores" histogram, the most frequent mean quality that the warn (≤ 27) and fail (≤ 20) thresholds test, and MultiQC's copy of the histogram are all shifted down by up to one unit, on average 0.5.

**Reproduction:** 2,000 reads of 100 bp with qualities drawn per position from a normal distribution (mean 38 − 0.15·position, SD 4, clipped to 2–41). The exact mean of the per-read means is 30.018; FastQC's histogram has mean 29.527, every bin equals the number of reads whose *floored* mean is that value, and none equals the rounded means. Script: `f1_basic_quality_modules.py` (library A) in the repository linked below.

**Fix:** sum into a `long` and round the mean (`Math.round(sum / (double) length)`). With that change the histogram equals the rounded per-read means and its mean is 30.02. PR follows.
