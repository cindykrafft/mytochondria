[COMMENT 4] GuangchuangYu (MEMBER), 2026-06-22T09:12:16Z (+1 x2)
Thanks for the report. We looked into this carefully and found that there are actually two separate issues behind the result differences.

1. ORA / enrichment workflows
For ORA-based workflows, part of the drift was caused by the backend migration to `enrichit`: zero-overlap gene sets were still included in the multiple-testing correction pool. That has now been fixed in `enrichit::ora_gson()` This explains a substantial part of the difference for ORA-like analyses.

2. GSEA workflows
For GSEA, the situation is more subtle. The enrichment scores themselves are already aligned very closely with `fgsea`, but the multilevel P-value estimation is Monte Carlo based, so single-run raw P-values can vary noticeably when `sampleSize` / `nPermSimple` are small. In other words, some of the observed drift is due to estimation variance rather than a systematic scoring error.

Thanks for pointing this out — it helped us tighten both the ORA and GSEA compatibility paths.
